# OpenBGA QM LIMS product

OpenBGA QM LIMS is part of [OpenBGA](https://github.com/openbga). The repository is [openbga/qm-lims](https://github.com/openbga/qm-lims).

Are you looking for support? Contact [bgasoft.com](https://bgasoft.com).

OpenBGA QM LIMS supports manufacturing quality-control laboratories in pharma/biotech, dietary supplements, food, chemical/industrial and medical devices in **one system**. Segment and destination-market obligations are controlled configuration, not separate products. One organization/site per deployment and online staff access are initial assumptions; neither a customer portal nor shared tenancy is part of this foundation.

The [regulatory matrix](regulations.md) is the authority for researched applicability, sources, jurisdiction and caveats, with a baseline of 2026-11-10. No environmental laboratory rule is inherited merely because code is reusable.

The platform and bounded native QC modules are under development. This is not a completed MVP or authorization for regulated production. The product supports a laboratory's quality system; it does not certify compliance with Part 11, cGMP, QMSR or ISO. Identity proofing, intended-use validation, qualified personnel, approved procedures and recovery remain deployment responsibilities. Laboratory approval, QC disposition and manufacturer release/QP certification are distinct decisions.

## Users and authority

| Role | Responsibility | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Analyst | Receive lots/samples, record observations and batch QC | Cannot review their own work |
| Reviewer | Review original records and results with PIN signature; accept a supplier's certificate result in place of a test; accept a stability pull with the outcome of its results | Must be independent of authorship; return/correction flows retain history |
| Quality | Approve configuration, keep the supplier register, qualify suppliers, approve contract laboratories, decide which requirement applies where the specifications of a lot differ, replace a lot's requirements during testing, make QC disposition decisions (release, rejection, and cancellation of a lot received in error), and record and accept the laboratory's intended use and its rehearsals | Cannot bypass missing/failing requirements or self-review |
| Admin | Read every laboratory area; manage named users and roles; reset another account's two-factor sign-in; control the sign-in announcement; record rehearsals of recovery, downtime and upgrades, as system administrator, for quality to accept | No scientific mutation or signing without its designated lab role; no self-granted additional roles or removal of the last active admin |
| Auditor | Read records and inspect audit history | No scientific mutation |

The initial roles map to explicit Django groups. Later receiving, supervisor and metrology responsibilities may be split without weakening independence. Qualifications belong to the relevant method/activity, not merely to group membership. Account ownership must remain individual; shared signers are prohibited. Superusers receive no implicit laboratory or web account-management role. Accounts without a recognized role reach an access-guidance page instead of a permission loop.

Account administrators can create users, update identity, assign/revoke roles, deactivate/reactivate, clear PIN lockout, require PIN re-enrollment, force password replacement and reset two-factor sign-in. Every administrative web change requires a reason and a fresh username/PIN signature bound to the resulting account revision. Initial and optional recovery passwords are entered privately and must be replaced before workspace access; no email delivery is assumed. Changes invalidate existing target sessions. Self-service password replacement requires the current password and a reason, with an audit event but no PIN, so credential recovery remains possible. The audited operator commands support bootstrap and controlled recovery; they are a separate trusted channel.

Account administrators also control one sign-in announcement: a notice shown on the sign-in page to anyone, before authentication. They write, publish and withdraw it with a reason and signature, and every version stays in the audit trail. It exists for operational notices and, on a synthetic demonstration instance, for listing shared demonstration accounts. Such accounts are not individual signers; an instance that publishes them holds demonstration data only, and must never list an account that can change the announcement. Accounts the demonstration seed publishes cannot change their own password or PIN, or set up two-factor sign-in.

**Two-factor sign-in.** Each person can set up an authenticator app (time-based one-time codes, RFC 6238) on **Two-factor sign-in**, reached from Account access: they confirm their password, add the key to the app from a QR code or by typing it, and confirm with a code the app shows. Ten one-time recovery codes are then shown once. From then on an accepted password does not sign the account in until a current code from the app, or an unused recovery code, is entered on a second step within five minutes. A code from the app works once; a wrong code counts toward the account's consecutive sign-in failures, like a wrong password, and while the account is locked even a correct code is refused. After 100 consecutive wrong codes, counted across locks, no code is accepted until an administrator resets two-factor sign-in; a correct password alone, on any page, does not clear that count. Making new recovery codes, setting up a new app or turning two-factor sign-in off needs the password and a current code. The deployment chooses which laboratory roles must use it (`MFA_REQUIRED_ROLES`, a list of roles or `all`; none by default): an account that must and has not set it up reaches only the two-factor page, password change, page help and sign-out until it does. A shared demonstration account cannot set it up and is not required to. An administrator who verified the person's identity resets another account's two-factor sign-in with a reason and a signature; the audited `reset_two_factor` command does the same from the operator shell, for when no other administrator can sign in. Every change advances the account revision and ends the account's other sessions, and each signature records how the signer's session signed in (`sign_in_factors`). The authenticator key is stored, because the server computes the same codes, and is shown only to its owner while setting up; recovery codes are stored as SHA-256 digests of 120 random bits. Codes from an app are not phishing-resistant. **Security keys and passkeys** (W3C Web Authentication) are the phishing-resistant second factor: an owner registers up to ten, each named, after confirming the password (and a current second factor once two-factor sign-in is on); the browser asks the key, which signs a fresh challenge bound to the site's host name and the page's origin, so an answer given to a look-alike site is refused. The first key turns two-factor sign-in on and comes with the recovery codes; an app and keys can be used side by side, the app can be removed while a key remains, and removing the last second factor turns two-factor sign-in off, which a required role refuses. At the second step and for every owner change, a key's answer is an alternative to a code: a refused answer counts like a wrong code, each challenge is answered once, and a signature count that does not increase is refused (a copy of the key may exist). Only the public key is stored; attestation is not requested, so the product does not restrict which make of key is used, and keys that can be copied to the owner's other devices (synced passkeys) are shown as such. A key works only at the host name it was registered at, over HTTPS or on `localhost`, not at an IP address. **Signing in with a passkey alone**: **Sign in with a passkey** on the sign-in page asks the browser for any key the person added, with no account named; the key must verify its holder (its own PIN, fingerprint or face), so it stands for both factors, and the key's credential identifies the account, and the answer must carry that account's user handle. A refused answer is not counted toward the account's lock or stop, because a key's signature cannot be guessed and counting unsigned answers would let anyone who has seen a key's credential ID lock the account without its password; an account that is locked or stopped refuses even a valid key. A password change does not remove keys, so after a suspected compromise the owner or an administrator also reviews the account's keys. The signature PIN is unchanged. A key that does not verify its holder still works as the second factor after the password. The deployment can turn this off (`PASSKEY_SIGN_IN`, on by default), so that every account enters its password first.

**Single sign-on.** Signing in through the organization's identity provider uses OpenID Connect (authorization code flow with PKCE, state and nonce) and is off unless the deployment configures one provider (`OIDC_ISSUER`, `OIDC_CLIENT_ID`, `OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET`). No account is created, activated or given roles from the provider: an owner who is signed in links their own account on **Organization sign-in** after confirming the password (and a second factor when two-factor sign-in is on), then authenticates at the provider. The link is the provider's issuer and subject, the only claims the standard makes stable; one organization account links to one laboratory account. From then on **Sign in with** the provider's name signs that account in without the password. The ID token must be signed by a key the provider publishes with an asymmetric algorithm and name this provider, this client alone as audience and the request's nonce. The provider is asked to authenticate the person at each sign-in (`OIDC_PROMPT=login` by default), so that a session someone else left open at the provider on a shared workstation is not used. The provider's sign-in stands for the password: an account with a second factor is still asked for it, `MFA_REQUIRED_ROLES` applies as before, signatures record `sign_in_factors` `["identity_provider"]` or `["identity_provider", <factor>]`, and the PIN is still entered for every signature. A refused answer is not counted toward the account's lock, and a locked or inactive account is refused. Linking and unlinking advance the account revision, end the account's other sessions and are recorded in the audit trail; an administrator removes another account's link with a reason and a signature, and a forced password reset or a two-factor reset removes it too, because whoever knew the old password could have linked their own organization account. An owner who changes their password is reminded of the link. The link shows the provider's subject beside the name it gave, and an email serves as that name only when the provider marks it verified. When the ID token states when the person authenticated, a time before the request is refused. The discovery document must name the configured issuer and HTTPS endpoints; only the keys the client needs are kept from it, so it cannot change the client's scopes or PKCE. Deactivating someone at the provider stops only this way in: their password still signs in until an administrator deactivates the laboratory account. Signing out ends the session here, not at the provider. SAML, accounts created from the provider, roles from provider groups and accepting the provider's own multi-factor check in place of the account's second factor are not implemented.

Accepted limitation: administrators are trusted to perform account administration. An admin who sets an initial or temporary password and requires PIN re-enrollment could act as that account. The application does not prevent this; laboratory SOPs and review of the audit trail (including `account.created`, `account.reset_password`, `account.reenroll_pin` and `account.reset_two_factor`) are the mitigations.

## Journeys

1. Receive an identified material/manufacturing lot in quarantine with its receipt details: the supplier or that it was made on site, the supplier's and manufacturer's lot numbers, the delivery document, the date, the quantity, the containers and what the examination on receipt found. Attach the approved effective rule revision, assign the approved specifications of every scope the lot falls under (the general one and named market or customer overlays) and a sampling plan, and record sampling provenance. Rule sets that name the lot's material, stage or scopes apply beside the lot's own, and where the specifications state a test differently quality decides with a signature which requirement applies. For a lot received from a supplier, record what the supplier's certificate of analysis states against those requirements; these stay the supplier's results. Where the rule set allows it and the supplier is qualified for the material and the test, a stated result can be accepted in place of the laboratory's own test.
2. Assign tests from the frozen requirements; record numeric, count or qualitative observations, or calculate a numeric result from raw inputs with the method's approved formula; record replicates and required batch QC with units, limits, the instruments and reagent or standard lots used, raw-data references or attached raw-data files, and attribution. Where an approved contract laboratory performed the test, record what its report states instead. Preserve applied rule/specification/method revisions.
3. Have a different qualified person inspect source data and audit history, then re-enter username and signing PIN to review the exact revision.
4. Have an authorized quality person evaluate completeness, failures and outstanding holds and sign the QC disposition. A laboratory decision never silently implies manufacturer batch release.
5. Issue a versioned CoA from approved evidence; amendment preserves the original and states the reason. Native issuance stores the evidence snapshot and PDF; later downloads return the issued bytes.

A failure creates a visible hold/investigation path. Retesting, resampling or invalidation must never erase the original result or allow testing into compliance. Stability protocols generate scheduled pulls. A pull is tested in the system against the acceptance criteria of its protocol, with the same recording, review and signatures as release testing, and its tests belong to the pull, so the record of a released lot does not change; a pull of a protocol without acceptance criteria in the system links a sample of a lot in quarantine or external evidence. Recording or correcting an observation or a batch QC result requires a current approved analyst qualification for the method, names the instruments and the reagent or standard lots used, and is refused when one of them is not fit for use. The evidence relied on is kept with the result. The separate resource-use page still records use against a sample outside native testing.

## In-app page documentation

Every staff page offers documentation from an icon at the top-right of the top bar. It opens a resizable right-side panel (a full-width overlay on narrow screens), follows navigation, and remembers its open/closed state and width in the browser. Each entry explains purpose, permissions, layout and every tab, action and field, naming `model.field` where applicable. It also explains hidden filters, defaults, permission conditions, transitions, validations and signature/audit effects. Documentation is derived from source, labels unverified policy as an assumption, and changes with the page. Automated coverage and reference checks are part of the release gate; prose accuracy remains the author’s responsibility.

Every signed-in person reaches **What's new** from the top bar. The public [roadmap](https://openbga.github.io/qm-lims/roadmap.html) groups the remaining and later work into capability areas, without dates. Its source is `website/roadmap.html`.

The native analytical contract supports three result types, each set on the approved specification line. **Numeric** results and **counts** (whole numbers of zero or more) are compared with a lower limit, an upper limit or both; each limit either includes or excludes its boundary. A declared reporting precision rounds the replicate mean half-up before comparison; `reporting_decimals=null` means exact comparison without rounding. Submission rejects a nonterminating mean or an exact mean requiring more than eight decimal places rather than silently rounding it. Individual replicate limits are checked when configured. A numeric or count result may be recorded as **less than a value** (for example `<10`). It is stored and reported as recorded and never becomes zero or a number. It conforms only when the line has no lower limit and the stated value does not exceed the upper limit; otherwise it cannot be shown to conform and is treated as not conforming. When any replicate is a less-than result no mean is calculated and every replicate must conform on its own. **Qualitative** results are chosen from a controlled pair (conforms or does not conform; absent or present) against the approved expected outcome and criterion, and each records what was observed. Every replicate must show the expected outcome. The less-than comparison and the replicate rules are project design defaults, not cited requirements (see REG-RT02 and REG-RT07 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md)); a quality owner approves them for the intended use. Greater-than results, method-specific rounding conventions and batch QC checks other than directly entered numeric values with inclusive limits are not supported. A qualitative line approved before expected outcomes existed is held until a new specification version states one. The software does not interpret acceptance criteria written as powers of ten; the approved line states the explicit maximum. No preset may silently bypass these boundaries.

A method version may define one **calculation**: a formula over 1 to 12 named inputs with units, using addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and parentheses, with the unit of the calculated value and the number of decimals it is rounded to. It is approved and frozen with the method version. For such a method the analyst enters the inputs instead of a value. The system calculates the replicate value, rounds it half-up to the approved decimals and stores the inputs as entered, the formula and the method version with the observation. Submission and review calculate again from the stored inputs and refuse a difference. A specification line that uses a calculated method must be numeric in the calculated unit; otherwise the requirement is held. A calculated result cannot be reported as less than a value, and batch QC checks are entered directly. Arithmetic carries 50 significant digits before rounding; rounding of intermediate values, significant figures and unit conversion are not applied. These are project design defaults, not cited requirements, and a quality owner approves each formula for its intended use.

A method version may also require that every result and batch QC entry records at least one instrument, at least one reagent or standard lot, or both. Whatever is selected is checked when the entry is recorded: equipment must be in service with an independently accepted, unexpired calibration and, where required, current qualification evidence; a reagent or standard lot and its source lots must be released and unexpired. The analyst needs a current approved qualification for the method, for batch QC as well. A refused entry stores nothing and the refusal is audited. The method does not say which instrument or lot is suitable; that remains the analyst's and the reviewer's judgement. After a failed calibration the equipment page lists the tests and QC batches that used the equipment since its last accepted passing calibration.

**Raw-data files** can be attached to an open test by an analyst. Each file is stored in PostgreSQL exactly as received with its SHA-256 checksum, can never be changed or deleted, and is returned unchanged on download after the checksum is verified. A file is limited to 10 MiB by default (`RAW_DATA_MAX_BYTES`) and a test to 20 files; a deployment can also cap all files together (`RAW_DATA_TOTAL_MAX_BYTES`). Files are not opened, converted or scanned by the software, and they are not part of the certificate.

**Receipt details** are part of receiving a lot: a lot is received with them or not at all. They state where the lot came from (a supplier from the register, or made on site), the supplier's and the manufacturer's lot numbers when known, the manufacturer when it is not the supplier, the delivery or batch document, the date received, the quantity with its unit, the number and kind of containers, and what the examination on receipt found. The details are never edited: while the lot is in quarantine an analyst corrects them by adding a record that states its reason and links to the one it replaces. A correction advances the lot's revision, so a disposition page opened before it must be reviewed again. Whoever recorded or corrected a lot's receipt details cannot sign its disposition. Quality keeps the **supplier register**, which records who a supplier is. It does not qualify a supplier, and naming a supplier does not reduce testing; a qualification is a separate signed record (see below). A discrepancy found on receipt is recorded and shown; it does not place a hold by itself. Lots received before receipt details were kept have none until an analyst records them. What the examination on receipt must cover and how suppliers are approved are decided by the laboratory's own procedures; the fields are project design defaults derived from REG-P17, REG-P28 and REG-S09 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md).

**Supplier certificates** record what a supplier's certificate of analysis states for a lot received from a supplier: its number, issuer and date, the lot number printed on it, where the document is kept, and for each of the lot's approved requirements that it reports, the result, the method and the limits as the certificate states them. An analyst records them while the lot is in quarantine and has its requirements assigned. Each result is compared with the lot's requirement as approved, exactly as stated and without rounding it first, and the requirement compared with is kept with the result. A result stated as less than a value stays as stated; a qualitative result keeps the certificate's wording beside the outcome chosen; units are not converted. These are the supplier's results and stay apart from the laboratory's own tests. Recording them answers no requirement and does not change which tests the lot needs; a stated result counts towards the disposition and is printed on the certificate of analysis only when it is accepted in place of a test, as described below. A supplier result that does not meet the specification is shown on the lot record and where the disposition is signed; it does not place a hold by itself. A lot can hold several certificates, with one current record for each certificate number. Records are never edited: a correction adds a linked record, a record made in error is withdrawn and kept, and each change advances the lot's revision. Whoever recorded, corrected or withdrew a lot's supplier certificate cannot sign its disposition. What a certificate must contain, who reviews it and whether a failing supplier result holds a lot are decided by the laboratory; the fields and the comparison are project design defaults.

**Reliance on a supplier's results** lets a result stated on a supplier's certificate of analysis stand in for the laboratory's own test of a lot. Three things must hold, and each is a signed decision of the laboratory, not of the software. The lot's approved rule set must allow it: a rule set may carry `supplier_results` with the list of tests the laboratory always performs itself (`own_tests`, each one of the rule set's required tests); a rule set without it allows no reliance, and no preset adds it. Quality must have qualified the supplier: a **supplier qualification** names one supplier, one material and the tests it covers, says how the reliability of the supplier's results was established, references the evidence and has a first and a last day; one quality person records it and a different one signs it, and quality can revoke it with a signature. And the result must be accepted on the lot: an analyst proposes one result of the lot's current certificate record, and a reviewer who neither proposed it, nor recorded or corrected that certificate, nor recorded or corrected the lot's receipt details signs the acceptance. A proposal is refused when the rule set keeps the test with the laboratory, when no signed qualification of the receipt's supplier for the lot's material covers the test on the day the lot was received, when the certificate record states no method or no limits for the result, when the stated result does not meet the lot's current requirement, when another current certificate of the lot states a result for the same test that does not meet it, or when the laboratory has already started testing that requirement (a result, a raw-data file, a QC batch or a decision on a test); a refusal by the rule set or for want of a qualification is kept in the lot's audit trail. An accepted result answers its requirement for the whole lot: no test is assigned for it, a test already assigned stays open and takes no observation, reported result, raw-data file or QC batch. While the lot is in quarantine a proposal can be withdrawn with a reason, and an acceptance, which carries a signature, with the signature of a reviewer or of quality; the laboratory then tests the requirement or the result is proposed again. Testing and acceptance exclude each other in both directions, so a failing laboratory result cannot be replaced by the supplier's. The accepted result stays the supplier's result: it is compared exactly as stated and is not rounded, it is listed apart from the laboratory's results where the disposition is signed, in the disposition evidence and on the certificate of analysis, which names the supplier's certificate, the supplier and the qualification. Whoever proposed it, and whoever recorded the certificate, cannot sign the lot's disposition. What an acceptance relied on is kept with it and is never rewritten: when the certificate record is corrected or withdrawn, another certificate of the lot states a failing result for the test, the receipt names another supplier or another date of receipt, the requirement changes or laboratory work appears on it, the acceptance stops standing and blocks the disposition until it is withdrawn. A qualification revoked later is relied on no further, and a proposal not yet signed under it no longer stands; acceptances already signed stand, say so where the disposition is signed, and are listed on the qualification for quality to assess under its own procedure. Sampling the lot stays required. The software does not decide which tests are identity tests, how often a supplier is confirmed again (the qualification's last day is the only limit; a renewal is a new signed qualification), how many lots must agree before testing is reduced, or which lots are tested in full; it does not compare a supplier's results with the laboratory's across lots. Qualifying per supplier, material and test, anchoring the qualification to the day of receipt, requiring a stated method and limits, and keeping acceptances signed before a revocation are project design defaults derived from REG-P31, REG-S10, REG-ICH09 and REG-EU10 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md); a quality owner confirms them for the intended use.

**Contract laboratory results** let a test of a lot be performed by an outside laboratory. Quality keeps a register of contract laboratories, which records who a laboratory is and approves nothing. For each method a laboratory may perform, one quality person records an approval with its evidence (the quality agreement, an audit report or an accreditation certificate) and the dates it is valid, and a different quality person signs it; quality can revoke it with a signature. Recording or signing an approval is not authorship of the results later reported under it. An analyst then records what the laboratory's report states for an open test: the laboratory, the report number and date, where the report is kept, and every replicate as reported. The entry is accepted only when an approved approval of that laboratory for the test's method covers the date of the report, and that approval is kept with the result. A refused entry stores nothing and the refusal is audited. From there the result is the test's result: the specification's replicates, rounding and limits apply, someone other than the person who recorded it reviews it against the report, a failing result opens an investigation, and the certificate of analysis names the laboratory and its report. The person who recorded it authored the result and cannot sign the lot's disposition. A test is tested in the laboratory or reported by a contract laboratory, never both, and a correction replaces the whole reported result with linked entries that keep the original. A correction of the same report keeps the approval already relied on; naming another laboratory or report is checked against an approval again. The contract laboratory's own batch QC, instruments, reagents, analysts and calculation inputs are not recorded: they stay in its report, which the reviewer checks. After a revocation no further report is accepted under the approval; results already recorded stay, say so on the test record and where the review and the disposition are signed, and are listed on the approval for quality to assess under its own procedure, which the software does not record. Approving per method, anchoring the approval to the date of the report and keeping results recorded before a revocation are project design defaults derived from REG-P29, REG-P30, REG-EU09 and REG-ICH08 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md); a quality owner confirms them for the intended use. The written agreement with the laboratory, its audits and the transfer of the method to it are managed outside the software.

**Exporting a lot's record.** Admin, quality and auditor, the roles that read the audit trail, can download a lot's complete record as one ZIP file from the lot page, to keep or to hand over for an inspection, an annual product review or a complaint. Everything is read from the database in one repeatable-read, read-only transaction, so every part describes the same instant. The package holds a printed copy (`record.pdf`) with every field of every record, each signature with the signer's name, date and time and meaning, and the audit trail with what each event changed; an electronic copy (`record.json`) with every stored field, its label, kind and choices, the signed content of each signature, the audit trail and its reviews; the raw-data files and issued certificates byte for byte; and `manifest.json` and `SHA256SUMS`, which standard tools check. The record covers the lot's own records (receipt details, supplier certificates and accepted supplier results, requirement assignments, samples and sampling records, stability studies and pulls, reserves, retention obligations and holds, tests including the tests of its stability pulls, observations, raw-data files, investigations, QC batches, certificates, and the instruments, reagent lots and contract laboratory reports used) and the configuration they name (rule sets, including every one the lot was under, specifications, methods and sampling plans with the versions frozen in its requirements, suppliers and their qualifications, equipment, reagent lots, contract laboratories and stability protocols) as it stands at the export, with its signatures but not its own history. The checksums of the stored files and the hashes stored with signatures, certificates, requirement assignments and approved versions are recomputed, and each outcome is printed; a mismatch is copied and reported, not hidden. The package is built during the request, so a lot whose raw-data files total several hundred MiB can exceed the server's request time limit. The exporter states a purpose. The export is recorded in the lot's audit trail with the package's SHA-256 and changes nothing else. A package is a copy produced by the software: whether it serves as a true copy for a laboratory's records is part of that laboratory's validation of this function.

**Revised versions** of a rule set, a specification, a method or a sampling plan are drafted, approved by a second quality person and effective-dated like the first. When a version is approved whose effective period overlaps an approved version of the same document, the earlier version is retired: it stops applying when the new one takes over, which is the new version's start or, if that has already passed, the moment the approval is signed. It is never retired retroactively, a version that had not started yet never applies, and a draft cannot retire a version numbered after it. The retired version keeps its approved content and signature and states which version replaced it and from when; the approval page lists these effects before the PIN is requested and each one is audited. A lot keeps the versions frozen when its requirements were assigned, so approving a new specification or method changes nothing for lots already received. The rule set is the exception that the existing gates make: assigning tests and the disposition need the lot's rule version to be in force, so a lot on a retired rule version has its requirements assigned again (before testing) or replaced (during testing) first. Results already recorded are still reviewed and an investigation is still concluded under the retired version, so that a failing result is never left without its review.

**Several sets of requirements on one lot.** A lot's requirements are assigned under one or more scopes: `general`, and named market or customer overlays such as `eu-market`. A scope is a name the laboratory gives a market or a customer; the laboratory enters which ones a lot is for. Every approved specification of the lot's material and stage for a named scope applies in full. A lot also falls under more than one rule set: its own, chosen when it is received, and every **additional rule set** in force whose approved policy says, under `applies_to`, that it applies to the lot's material, to its stage or to one of its scopes (a dimension the policy leaves out matches every lot). An additional rule set is never chosen at receipt, and a rule set does not change between the two kinds from one version to the next. The site is not a dimension: one deployment serves one site. Nothing is merged. A test that the specifications state alike (name, acceptance criterion, rounding, replicates, method version and whether it is required) is one requirement and keeps the specifications that state it. A test stated by one specification only applies as stated. Every rule set's required tests must be among the lot's required tests, and a supplier's result can stand in for a test only when every rule set the lot is under allows it. The lot is placed on an applicability hold, and is neither sampled nor tested, when its requirements are incomplete (a named scope has neither an approved specification nor an additional rule set, a specification or a method has no version in force, or a rule set requires a test that no specification applying to the lot requires) and when they conflict: two specifications state a test differently. The software then picks neither the wider nor the stricter statement. A quality person who recorded none of the lot's data reviews every statement and what the statements differ in, chooses for each such test the one that applies to this lot, gives a reason and signs with the meaning requirements decided. The page shows everything the signature freezes (the specifications and rule sets in force and the requirements stated one way), and a specification, method or rule set version approved after it was opened refuses the signature until it is reviewed again. The statements not chosen stay in the record with the decision and with who decided, when and why; they are shown where the disposition is signed and are printed on the certificate of analysis. A decision settles only such differences: anything incomplete is resolved in the configuration first. Requirements assigned again are decided again; once quality has decided, only quality assigns them again, and a decision set aside that way is named where the disposition is signed. Once testing has started the same choice is part of the signed replacement, which carries a choice forward only while the scope that was named still states the test exactly as it is frozen for the lot; otherwise the choice is made again. Entering a lot's scopes and signing this decision are not authorship of the lot's data: the person who entered the scopes may sign the decision, and the person who decided may sign the disposition. Assigning tests and the disposition need every rule set version the lot is under to be in force, the additional ones included. The binding rules read ask that a lot meets every specification that applies to it (REG-CFG09 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md)), and none says how a difference between two of them is resolved. The decision therefore records which requirement the lot is tested against and why; it is not a waiver of a specification that binds the lot, and the signer chooses a statement under which a passing result also meets the others or states why another specification does not bind this lot. The software does not check that, does not decide which specification binds a lot, and does not evaluate one result against each differing specification. Counting a requirement stated alike once, the holds, and a decision for one test of one lot by an independent signer are project design defaults derived from REG-CFG09 to REG-CFG11; a quality owner confirms them for the intended use.

**Replacing a lot's requirements.** Before testing starts, an analyst or quality assigns a lot's requirements again and the versions then in force are frozen. Once a test exists, only a signed replacement changes them: a quality person who recorded none of the lot's receipt, supplier certificate, sampling or test data, and proposed no supplier result for it, reviews what the versions now in force would change, states a reason and signs with the meaning requirements replaced. The replacement freezes the versions in force for the same material, stage and scopes, the version in force of the lot's own rule set and the additional rule sets that apply then; the sampling plan is kept, because the lot's samples were taken under it. A test carries over when its requirement is exactly the same (name, acceptance criterion, rounding, replicates and method version). Every other test is kept with everything recorded on it and marked superseded: it takes no more work, never counts towards the disposition, and is repeated as a linked test under the new requirement when the analyst assigns the lot's tests again. The earlier requirements stay in the record, and the replacement keeps what it changed and which tests it affected. Nothing recorded is set aside unevaluated: a replacement is refused while an investigation is open on the lot and while a test that would be superseded holds results or raw-data files that were not submitted, a confirmed out-of-specification result keeps blocking the lot whatever requirements apply later, and laboratory work on a superseded test still excludes a supplier's result for that requirement. A supplier result accepted against the replaced requirements stops standing and is withdrawn and proposed again. A replacement that cannot be resolved completely (no version in force, or a rule set the specification does not cover) is refused with the reasons and records no hold. The disposition page tells the signer about each replacement and the tests it superseded. A recorded result is not evaluated again against revised limits, the sampling plan is not replaced, and the scope of a lot is not changed by a replacement; whether a change must be applied to lots in testing, and the impact assessment behind it, stay in the laboratory's change control. These are project design defaults derived from REG-CFG06 to REG-CFG08 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md); a quality owner confirms them for the intended use.

**Backup and restore checks.** `backup_manifest` records, when a backup is taken, each table's row count and a fingerprint of its rows, the schema, the migrations and the sequence positions, read in one read-only snapshot; with `--dump` it runs `pg_dump` from that same snapshot. After the archive is restored into a new database, `verify_restore` compares the copy with the manifest, recomputes the signature hashes and the checksums of issued certificates and raw-data files, checks that no history trigger is disabled and that the release's migrations and system checks pass, and writes a report that the operator keeps as the evidence of the restore test. A copy passes only when it equals the manifest. Neither command writes to the database, and neither file holds record content or credentials. Scheduling, off-site and encrypted storage, retention and RPO/RTO stay with the laboratory. The procedure is in [operations](operations.md).

**Validation pack.** Every product requirement in the table above is traced in `docs/traceability.toml` to the automated tests that give evidence for it, and the application suite refuses a requirement without a test, an entry that does not exist or a test that is not traced. After the application and browser suites and the container smoke test pass, CI builds the pack of the commit: the requirements, for each one the tests traced to it with their outcomes, each suite's JUnit results and environment (Python, PostgreSQL server, installed packages, Chromium), the release notes and a SHA-256 of every file. A requirement passes when a traced test passed, none failed, all ran and none was skipped without a reason the map allows; the pack passes only when every requirement passed, every result is traced and the tests ran on the commit without uncommitted changes. The same command builds the pack in a laboratory's own environment. The pack is supplier evidence that a laboratory can review and assess as part of its own risk-based validation (EU GMP Annex 11 sections 3.3, 4.4 and 4.5; 21 CFR 11.10(a) places validation on the regulated user); it does not test a deployment's configuration or intended use, and a passing requirement means the traced tests passed, not that the requirement is met in full.

**Acceptance journeys.** `python manage.py acceptance_journeys --database NAME --output DIR --operator NAME` runs five journeys end to end through the application's services, each on its own synthetic lot: a lot released and certified; a failing result investigated, retested once, confirmed and the lot rejected; a receipt and an observation corrected, with the originals kept, and the lot released with the corrected value; a lot rejected on receipt before testing and a duplicate receipt cancelled; and a certificate amended while version 1 stays byte for byte. Each step states what is expected and what was observed and names the product requirements it gives evidence for, and refusals are steps too: an author approving their own rule set, an observation without a value, self-review (by an analyst who also holds the reviewer role), a review signed with a wrong PIN (the failed attempt is counted and audited although the review is refused), a disposition or rejection by someone who received the lot (who also holds the quality role), disposition and rejection while an investigation is open, a second retest beyond the specification, invalidation when the retest also fails, requirements, a disposition or a certificate for a rejected lot, an amendment from a stale view, and direct changes in the database to an issued certificate, an observation, a signature and an audit event. It first creates eight synthetic accounts, with random passwords and PINs held in memory and never shown (only their hashes are stored), and the configuration the journeys use, each approved by someone other than its author. Because signing checks the PIN outside any enclosing transaction, so that a failed attempt is kept, the run commits its records like the pages do: it refuses a database other than the one `--database` names, one that holds any account, or one not migrated to exactly this release, and runs in a new database created for it. Each journey ends with the export of its lot's record. The evidence folder holds a printable `report.html` and `report.json` (the run, the release from `SOURCE_REVISION` or `RENDER_GIT_COMMIT`, the PostgreSQL server, each step with its outcome), the lot record packages, the certificates and a `SHA256SUMS`; a failed step stops its journey and the others run (a failed configuration step stops them all), the report is written and the command exits nonzero. CI runs it with the application image of every commit, and the validation pack includes the folder after checking its checksums and that it ran on the pack's commit. The run uses synthetic configuration and the services rather than a browser: it is evidence that the release behaves as the journeys expect in that environment, for the laboratory to assess as a test tool, not a test of the laboratory's own configuration or intended use (EU GMP Annex 11 section 4.7; PIC/S PI 011-3 section 13.4 leaves the tests of the user requirements to the regulated user).

**Validation records.** The laboratory records its own validation decisions under **Validation**. A record of the intended use states what the system is used for and what is outside it, the supported assays (methods with an approved version in force), the process owner and the system owner, other responsibilities, the laboratory's validation report and the known issues and conditions of use. One quality person records it and a different one accepts it with a signature, which freezes every approved rule set, specification, method, sampling plan and stability protocol version in force, the version of each supported assay and the release that is running (`SOURCE_REVISION`, or `RENDER_GIT_COMMIT` on Render). The signing page is refused as stale when any of these, or the acceptance it would supersede, changed after it was opened. At most one record is accepted at a time, and accepting a newer one supersedes it. The intended-use list and the accepted record then show each approved version in force that differs from the frozen one, the methods in force that are not supported assays, and a different running release, for the laboratory to assess under its change control; no work is held because of them. Rehearsals of recovery from backup, of downtime with reconciliation of the records made meanwhile, and of upgrades are recorded after they took place, by quality or the system administrator: the procedure, acceptance criteria, observations and outcome (criteria met or not met, with the issues and actions when not met). The recorder attaches the evidence, such as the `verify_restore` report, stored unchanged with its checksum, and a quality person who did not record the rehearsal accepts it with a signature, whatever its outcome; the signature freezes the list of evidence files with their checksums. When the deployment states no release, the record says the release cannot be compared rather than calling it unchanged. Neither record is edited: one that will not be accepted is withdrawn with a reason and recorded again. The restore check verifies the evidence files with the other stored files. Keeping these records in the software, their fields and the frozen configuration are product design choices: the sources require validation, controlled changes to the configuration, restore checks and tested continuity arrangements, not a record in the system (REG-E02, REG-EU04, REG-EU06, REG-CFG12 and the note of 2026-11-17 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md)). How often to rehearse, the RTO and RPO, and whether a change calls for a new acceptance stay with the laboratory. The procedure is in [operations](operations.md#validation-records-of-the-laboratory).

**A test that cannot be completed.** An open test whose sample was lost, damaged or used up, or that cannot be finished because of an error in preparing or running it, an equipment, reagent or standard failure or another stated cause, is recorded as not completed from the test's page by a quality person who recorded, submitted or attached none of its data (and, for a test of an investigation, who could conclude that investigation), with the cause and a reason, re-entering username and PIN (meaning `not_completed`). A test awaiting review is reviewed or returned instead. Nothing recorded on the test is changed or set aside: the page shows, and the signature freezes, the readings as recorded, those outside the limits, every corrected reading with what replaced it and why (nobody reviews a test that is not submitted), the raw-data files and the QC batch, and the test then takes no more work, blocks nothing and answers nothing. What follows depends on the test, not on the signer: a test authorized by an investigation (a retest or resample, or its rerun) is left to that investigation, which can then be concluded or authorize another retest; current readings outside the limits, judged as recorded and as they would be reported once rounded to the reporting precision (and, when every replicate is recorded, as the result submission would calculate), open an investigation on the test, so that they are investigated and never discarded; any other test is repeated by a linked test of the kind "repeat" under the same frozen requirement, on the same sample, on the material of a stability pull, or on a sample of the lot recorded after the test was assigned that then stands in for the one sample it replaces; the lot then no longer requires tests of the chosen sample's own. A routine sample recorded before the test is never offered, but one recorded later is, so the page tells the signer to choose the replacement sample, and the signature records the choice. An authorized retest recorded as not completed before it held any reading, correction or raw-data file does not use the specification's retest allowance; one that held any does, and an investigation is not concluded as invalidated while one of its tests was recorded as not completed with a reading outside the limits, so the record cannot be used to test into compliance. The lot's evidence and the certificate's frozen evidence list the test with its cause and signature. A pull whose test is repeated is accepted on the repeat, and work recorded as not completed no longer keeps a study from being closed. The cause list, the requirement for quality to sign, refusing the record to the test's data authors, the automatic repeat and not counting a retest that held nothing are project design defaults that a quality owner confirms for the intended use (see the dated note in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md)).

**Rejecting a lot.** A lot in quarantine that fails its requirements or cannot be used is rejected from its record by a quality person who recorded none of its receipt details, supplier certificates, sampling or test data and proposed none of its supplier results, the same independence as the release. The signer chooses the grounds (results, condition or documents on receipt, the supplier, or another reason), states the reason and may name the deviation or nonconformance record of the quality system, re-entering username and PIN. The lot need not be fully tested: the signature freezes what stood in the way of release, the lot's tests as they stood, its investigations, its current receipt details, supplier certificates, supplier results and active holds, and the lot then takes no more receipt, sampling, certificate or test records; a hold or a reserve sample can still be recorded. An investigation still open on the lot is concluded first, and a recorded result outside its limits that was not submitted is submitted first, so a failure is always evaluated and investigated whatever is decided about the lot; results within their limits on a test that cannot be finished are kept as recorded. An authorized retest that cannot be run, or a test that cannot be completed while a recorded reading is outside its limits, is recorded as not completed (below) so that its investigation can be concluded. A failed QC batch that holds a test of a rejected lot can still be rejected; that test stays as the rejection froze it. No certificate of analysis is issued for a rejected lot, and a stability study does not start on one. Physical segregation and what happens to the material (return, destruction or reprocessing under the manufacturer's procedures) are decided and recorded in the quality system.

**Cancelling a lot received in error.** A lot recorded by mistake, such as a delivery recorded twice, is cancelled by a quality person other than whoever received it or made its records, with a reason and a signature, while nothing has been done with its material: no sample, test, reserve sample or stability study, and no supplier result standing in place of a test. The lot and every record stay, marked cancelled, its number is not used again, and it takes no reserve sample, retention obligation, hold or study. A lot whose material was sampled or kept is rejected instead.

New QC batches offer the method versions that apply now, and an earlier version only while tests in progress are frozen to it, so that a test a lot has started under its frozen method can still be run and evaluated. A test frozen to an earlier version cannot use a batch of a newer one. A QC batch that holds a superseded test is not fixed by it unless that test had been submitted. Once a lot has been sampled it keeps the sampling plan version its samples were taken under, also when its requirements are assigned again before testing.

Production QC disposition requires a native assignment and passing analytical/hold checks. The original manual-limit result path is DEBUG-only and unavailable once a lot has native assignments; mixed evidence blocks disposition. Released lots cannot accept new scientific results; their stability studies are tested on the pull, as described next, without reopening the lot.

**Stability testing in the system.** A stability protocol names, when it is created, the specification whose approved version states the acceptance criteria of its tests: a separate shelf-life specification or the one used for release, as the laboratory decides. Its approval is refused while the version in force does not state every test of every time point with a method version in force, and a study of it starts only on a lot whose assigned requirements are of that specification's material. Once a pull has been recorded, an analyst assigns its tests: one for each test of the time point, each freezing the acceptance criterion and method version in force at that moment, so a later version of the specification applies to later pulls. These tests belong to the pull and name no lot or sample. They are recorded, corrected, batched with QC, reported by a contract laboratory, submitted, reviewed by someone who recorded none of the data and investigated exactly like release tests; the analyst needs a qualification for the method and the instruments and lots used are checked. Every change to them advances the pull's revision, never the lot's. The lot's record, its samples, tests, investigations, gates, disposition evidence and certificates are not changed by its stability studies. A failing result opens an investigation that holds the pull until quality concludes it; the material pulled can be retested under that investigation, but a pull is not resampled. A reviewer who neither pulled, linked evidence nor produced or investigated any of the pull's data accepts it once every test has a reviewed result and no investigation is open: the signature records whether the reviewed results meet the acceptance criteria and freezes them with the pull. A confirmed failure is an outcome, not a missing pull: the pull is accepted with the outcome that the criteria were not met, and the study can still be completed. A study is not closed while a test of its pulls awaits review, holds results that were not submitted or is under an open investigation, so nothing recorded is left unevaluated. A pull that was taken but cannot be tested (lost or insufficient material) is recorded as missed with a reason when nothing of its testing is in progress, it is not ready for acceptance and none of its results failed; its conforming reviewed results stay in the record. A pull with a failing result is never recorded as missed: it is accepted with its outcome or, if a test of it cannot be completed, the study is terminated. The tests of a pull freeze the criteria the analyst was shown: a specification version approved after the page was opened refuses the assignment until the criteria are reviewed again. A draft protocol that will not be approved, for example because its specification cannot state one of its tests, is withdrawn by its author or by quality so that the next version of the code can be drafted. The study shows the results of each storage condition by time point beside the criterion each was judged against. The software places no hold on the lot, does not extend an investigation to other batches, does not detect trends or a significant change from the initial value, does not evaluate a shelf life and makes no report to an authority; these stay with quality under its own procedures (see REG-P32, REG-P33 and REG-CFG13 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md)). A pull is tested in the system or linked to a sample of a lot in quarantine or to external evidence, never both, and a protocol without a specification keeps the linked evidence. Naming one specification, freezing its version when a pull's tests are assigned, accepting a pull with a failing outcome and allowing a retest but no resample are project design defaults that a quality owner confirms for the intended use.

Five editable segment presets are examples requiring quality-owner review. They are not authoritative legal policies, complete panels or production defaults. Universal audit, signature linkage and no-self-review cannot be disabled by a preset. Unresolved applicability must hold decisions; a generic preset cannot authorize a specialized assay. Deferred native functionality requires a controlled external process before the corresponding regulated work is supported.
