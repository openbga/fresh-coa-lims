# Manufacturing QC laboratory regulations and LIMS requirements

Research date and access date: **2026-11-10**. Jurisdictions: United States, European Union and United Kingdom; international standards are identified separately. This is a product requirements baseline, not a legal opinion, certification, or a claim that OpenBGA QM LIMS or its users will be compliant. Software controls support a laboratory's procedures, qualified people, validation and quality system; they do not replace them.

## Confirmed scope: one system, configured obligations

**Design decision: one manufacturing QC LIMS serves pharma/biotech cGMP, supplements, food, chemical/industrial and devices. Segment rules are configuration in that system. Audit trails and PIN-based electronic signatures are core across every segment.** The shared workflow is incoming materials, in-process and finished-product samples, approved specifications/methods, testing, investigations, review and an authorized QC decision. Approval of laboratory results and manufacturer batch release remain distinct decisions.

The product rationale is a shared record/workflow model with explicit differences in obligations. Supplements have different identity-testing, sampling and retention rules ([§111.75][s75], [§111.605][s605]); food has hazard-dependent verification and a Part 11 exemption for Part 117-only records ([§117.165][f165], [§117.305(g)][f305]); devices use QMSR ([§820.10][d10]). Apply approved rule sets by product/material, activity, site and destination market, with method/customer/accreditation overlays where relevant. A record may carry several applicable obligations; an organization-wide industry label alone is insufficient. Preserve the rule-set revision evaluated with each decision, and require quality-owner resolution when rules conflict. These are derived product requirements, not claims that regulations mandate a particular configuration architecture.

PIN entry is the chosen signing interaction. For covered Part 11 use, the design must still satisfy unique verified signer identity, nonbiometric signature components/session controls and credential safeguards ([§11.100][e100], [§11.200][e200], [§11.300][e300]). Re-entering an arbitrary PIN, or clicking approval in a logged-in session, is not evidence by itself that those conditions are met. Configure the signing policy and validate the actual authentication/signing flow; retain signer, meaning, execution timestamp and permanent record-version linkage ([§11.50][e50], [§11.70][e70]). Food-only and industrial records receive the same core product controls by design decision even where Part 11 is not their legal basis.

This review establishes cross-segment regulatory requirements, not complete method or product-family coverage. Sterile/biologic assays, animal testing, radiopharmaceuticals and specialized manufacturing programs need their corresponding method and regulatory assessments before operational use; they are not excluded industries or separate products. Clinical testing and nonclinical safety studies require separate applicability research if requested.

## How to read the matrix

**M** = binding rule for the listed in-scope activity; **E** = GMP guideline expectation under the applicable regional framework; **G** = nonbinding guidance/recommended approach; **C** = conditional accreditation, certification, contractual or adopted-quality-system requirement; **DRAFT** = proposed text, not an effective obligation. These labels describe the source obligation, not a mandate to buy or implement a particular software feature. FDA guidance generally permits an alternative approach meeting applicable law ([FDA data-integrity guidance, introduction][di]).

**U** marks a verification limitation. Other entries were checked against the cited primary publication or the issuing authority's own public assessment material. For ISO 17025 the public NATA worksheet verifies the clause mapping; it is not a replacement for the licensed ISO text and does not make NATA-specific policies universal. Detailed current ISO 9001 and internal IATF clause mappings remain flagged below.

**MVP** means shared functionality and configuration needed across all confirmed segments, or an explicit controlled link to the responsible external system. **Later** means a specialized native capability, advanced automation or an unverified/draft extension; it never means an entire confirmed segment is deferred to another product. A mandatory obligation with a Later native module still needs an existing controlled process/system before the applicable activity is supported. MVP record links to ERP, document control, training or QMS need stable identifiers, controlled revisions and retrievable evidence. Legal applicability remains conditional even where the user has made a stronger control universal product policy. No priority designation authorizes regulated production use.

## Verification and limits

Roadmap gap review, accessed **2026-11-16**. These findings scope planned work; they do not describe implemented controls. For US finished drugs, [21 CFR 211.165(f)][p165] addresses rejection of failing products, [211.87][p87] addresses retesting or reexamining approved components, containers and closures after storage or adverse exposure, and [211.192][p192] extends an investigation to associated batches and products, including those already distributed. These duties do not prescribe the proposed software states, an automatic recall or a universal retest interval. Signed rejection, linked material reassessment and a quality-owned impact assessment with certificate withdrawal are project design choices; manufacturer release, reprocessing, recall and authority reporting stay in the manufacturer's quality system.

For adopted or accredited ISO/IEC 17025 scopes, the [NATA assessment worksheet][nata17025] (September 2025; international-standard assessment material from the Australian accreditor) maps activity-specific authorizations in 6.2.6, environmental conditions in 6.3, sampling/item handling in 7.3–7.4, continuing assurance including proficiency testing and interlaboratory comparisons in 7.7, and assessment of earlier results and customer notification where necessary in 7.10. [ILAC P9:01/2024][ilacp9] is policy for accreditation bodies on proficiency testing and other interlaboratory comparisons. These are not universal duties for every manufacturing segment; the full licensed ISO text was not read. Proposed sample acceptance, holding-time and aliquot records, scoped review authority, resource suitability and reviewed comparison outcomes require intended-use scoping.

For EU GMP computerized systems, [Annex 11][eu11] sections 1 and 4 address lifecycle risk assessment and validation, section 11 periodic evaluation, section 16 continuity arrangements and section 17 archival retrieval, including after system changes. The roadmap therefore makes laboratory-owned acceptance evidence, periodic reviews and archive/retirement assurance explicit. Existing backup checks, record copies and release test evidence remain implemented; the new planning entries do not validate a deployment or replace the laboratory's procedures.

The supplier-certificate record (product requirement OQ-006) was checked on 2026-11-14 against 21 CFR 211.84(d)(2)-(3) and 111.75(a)(2)(ii), read from the eCFR with Title 21 current through 2026-11-12 ([§211.84][p84], [§111.75][s75]; United States, rows REG-P03, REG-S02 and REG-S03). Both sections let a manufacturer accept a supplier's report in place of its own testing only under conditions: for drug components at least one specific identity test by the manufacturer and the reliability of the supplier's analyses established by validation at appropriate intervals; for dietary supplement components a supplier first qualified by confirming its results, periodic re-confirmation, quality control approval of that basis, and a certificate that includes a description of the methods used, the limits and the actual results. The software records the method, the limits and the result a certificate states and compares the result with the lot's approved requirement. It holds no supplier qualification or reliability evidence, so a recorded supplier result does not replace a test. Comparing the stated value without rounding, and showing a supplier result that does not meet the specification without placing a hold, are project design defaults; the sections read do not address either. Food supplier verification under 21 CFR part 117 subpart G and EU or ICH Q7 provisions on supplier certificates were not reviewed for this record.

The contract-laboratory record (product requirement OQ-006) was checked on 2026-11-14 against the sources of rows REG-P29, REG-P30, REG-EU09 and REG-ICH08 and against REG-ISO01 and REG-ISO03. 21 CFR 211.22(a) and 200.10 were read from the eCFR with Title 21 current through 2026-11-12 (United States). The rest of part 211 says nothing specific about testing by an outside laboratory, and no section of 21 CFR part 111 mentions a contract or outside laboratory; the general laboratory and quality control sections of both parts apply whoever performs the test. The expectations that a contract laboratory is evaluated before use, works under a written agreement and has its results reviewed come from the FDA quality-agreement guidance (nonbinding; United States), EU GMP Chapter 7 (revision 1, in operation since 2013-01-31; European Union) and ICH Q7 section 16 (FDA edition of September 2016). For ISO/IEC 17025 the public NATA worksheet states clause 6.6.2 (criteria for the evaluation, selection, monitoring and re-evaluation of external providers) and clause 7.8.2.1(p) (clear identification when results are from external providers); the licensed text was not read and the clauses bind only an accredited or adopting laboratory. The software holds a register of contract laboratories and, for each method, an approval with an evidence reference and dates that a second quality person signs. It accepts a reported result only under an approval that covers the date of the report, keeps the result in the laboratory's review and disposition path, and names the laboratory and its report on the certificate of analysis. Approving per method, anchoring the approval to the date of the report, refusing further reliance on a revoked approval while keeping results already recorded, and leaving the contract laboratory's batch QC, instruments and raw data in its own report are project design defaults; none of the sources read prescribes them. The written agreement, the audit programme and the transfer of the method to the contract laboratory are not held by the software. 21 CFR 211.34 (consultants), part 117, the QMSR purchasing controls and the 2007 preamble to part 111 were not reviewed for this record.

Reliance on a supplier's results (product requirement OQ-006) was checked on 2026-11-15 against 21 CFR 211.84(d)(1)-(3) and (e), 211.22(a), 111.75(a)(1)-(2), 111.77, 111.95(b)(2) and 111.105(b), read from the eCFR with Title 21 current through 2026-11-12 (United States; rows REG-P31 and REG-S10), ICH Q7 paragraphs 7.11-7.12 and 7.30-7.32 in the FDA edition of September 2016 and the same paragraphs of EU GMP Part II (row REG-ICH09), and EU GMP Part I Chapter 5 paragraphs 5.27 and 5.35-5.36 (in operation since 2015-03-01) with Annex 8 paragraphs 2-3 (European Union; row REG-EU10). Both United States rules are binding and keep identity testing with the manufacturer: for every drug component, and for every dietary ingredient of a supplement, while a supplement component that is not a dietary ingredient may have its identity confirmed from the certificate. ICH Q7 and EU GMP are guidance and guideline text and likewise keep an identity test on each batch. None of these texts fixes how often a supplier's results are confirmed again: they say "at appropriate intervals", "periodically" or "at appropriate intervals based on risk", and FDA's question-and-answer page leaves the validation procedure to the manufacturer. The only count is the ICH Q7 recommendation of complete analyses on at least three batches before in-house testing is reduced. None says whether the date of receipt, of the certificate or of use decides whether a qualification applies; they require that qualification come before reliance and that the determination come before use. The software holds a supplier qualification for one supplier, one material and named tests, with how the supplier was qualified, an evidence reference and dates, recorded by one quality person and signed by another, and revocable with a signature. A result stated on a supplier's certificate answers a requirement of a lot only when the lot's approved rule set allows reliance and does not keep that test with the laboratory, the qualification covers the day the lot was received, the certificate record is current and states the method and the limits, the stated result meets the lot's requirement and no other certificate of the lot contradicts it, the laboratory has not started testing that requirement, and a reviewer who authored none of it signs the acceptance. The accepted result stays the supplier's: it is listed apart from the laboratory's results in the disposition evidence and on the certificate of analysis. Qualifying per supplier, material and test, anchoring the qualification to the date of receipt, expressing the interval of re-confirmation only as the qualification's end date, requiring a stated method and limits in every segment, refusing a stated result that does not meet the specification, the second quality signature on the qualification, the independent reviewer on each acceptance, and keeping acceptances signed before a revocation while listing them for assessment are project design defaults; none of the sources read prescribes them. The software does not decide which tests are identity tests, does not count confirmed lots, does not compare a supplier's results with the laboratory's across lots and does not model an FDA-granted exemption or the testing exemption of ICH Q7 paragraph 7.32. It does not implement the food supply-chain program of 21 CFR part 117 subpart G (117.405-117.475 read): that is a hazard-based program of supplier approval before receipt and of verification activities, which a qualification of certificate results is not. For devices, 21 CFR 820.10 incorporates ISO 13485:2016, whose purchasing clauses were not read. The 1978 preamble to part 211, the full 2007 preamble to part 111, EU directives and EMA questions and answers were not reviewed.

Supersession of approved versions and the replacement of a lot's requirements during testing (product requirements OQ-001 and OQ-009) were checked on 2026-11-15 against 21 CFR 211.160(a), 211.100, 211.165, 211.84, 211.192, 111.70, 111.75, 111.105, 111.110, 111.315 and 111.320, read from the eCFR with Title 21 current through 2026-11-12 (United States); EU GMP Part I Chapter 4 (revision in operation since 2011-06-30), Chapter 6 (in operation since 2014-10-01) and Annex 11 (European Union); ICH Q7 sections 6, 11 and 13 in the FDA edition of September 2016; the FDA guidance on out-of-specification results (revision 1, May 2022); and ISO/IEC 17025:2017 clauses 7.2.1.3 and 8.3.2 as mapped in the public NATA worksheet (the ISO text was not read). Rows REG-CFG06 to REG-CFG08 record the result. The binding rules require that changes to specifications, sampling plans and test procedures are approved by the quality unit and that deviations are recorded and justified. None of the sources states which version of a specification or method applies to a lot that is already being tested, and none requires such a lot to be tested again or forbids keeping a test whose requirement did not change. The EU Chapter 4 edition in operation does not contain wording on preventing the use of superseded documents; that expectation is in ICH Q7 6.11 and in the ISO/IEC 17025 mapping. The following are therefore design defaults of the project, for a quality owner to confirm for the intended use: an earlier version is retired automatically when a later one that overlaps it is approved, and never before that approval; a lot keeps its frozen versions until quality replaces them with a signature; a test carries over only when its requirement is exactly the same, and any other test is superseded and repeated instead of being evaluated again against the new limits; the sampling plan is kept because the samples were taken under it; and a replacement is refused while an investigation is open or results are not submitted. Whether a change must be applied to lots in testing, and the impact assessment behind it, belong to the laboratory's change control, which the software does not record.

Applying several specifications and rule sets to one lot, and the decision where they state a test differently (product requirements OQ-001, OQ-009 and OQ-015), were checked on 2026-11-15 against 21 CFR 211.22, 211.84, 211.160, 211.165 and 211.194, 111.70, 111.73, 111.75, 111.77, 111.105, 111.113, 111.123 and 111.140, read from the eCFR with Title 21 current through 2026-11-12, and a word search of part 117 (United States); EU GMP Part I Chapters 1, 2, 4 and 6 (European Union); ICH Q7 sections 2 and 11 in the FDA edition of September 2016 and ICH Q6A; ISO/IEC 17025:2017 clauses 7.1 and 7.8.6 as mapped in the public NATA worksheet and quoted in ILAC-G8:09/2019 (the ISO text was not read); and 21 U.S.C. 351(b) and 381(e)(1) in the 2024 edition of the Code. Rows REG-CFG09 to REG-CFG11 record the result. The binding rules read ask that a lot conforms to all, or each, appropriate specification and give authority over specifications to quality. Part 117 does not use the word specification. No source read names a market or customer specification in the CFR, says how several specifications are combined, prescribes how a difference between two of them is resolved, or says that the stricter one applies automatically. Counting a requirement stated alike once, selecting neither of two differing requirements, the hold, the signed decision for one test of one lot by a person who recorded none of its data, and both holds for incomplete applicability are therefore design choices of the project, which a quality owner confirms for the intended use. Security keys and passkeys as a second sign-in factor (OQ-004, OQ-016) were checked on 2026-11-16 against NIST SP 800-63B-4 (August 2025; United States federal guideline, read on the access date) and W3C Web Authentication Level 3 (W3C Recommendation of 25 August 2026; international web standard).
