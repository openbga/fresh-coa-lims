Roadmap

What OpenBGA QM LIMS does today, what we are building next and what is planned for later.

The roadmap has no dates. It groups the work into capability areas and says which come first. Priorities can change as laboratories use the software and report what they need.

The areas come from three sources: the work a manufacturing QC laboratory does between receiving a lot and issuing its certificate, the regulations and standards those laboratories follow (see the regulatory matrix), and the limits recorded in the product documentation.

Done

Available today

A lot can go from receipt to a signed certificate of analysis with numeric, count and qualitative tests, including results calculated from raw measurements. These areas are in the current source and are tested on PostgreSQL and in browser journeys.

  • Approved configuration

    Rule sets, specifications, methods and sampling plans are versioned and effective-dated. Someone other than the author signs each approval. When a later version that overlaps an approved one is approved, the earlier version is retired from the new version's start and keeps its approved content and signature. Every lot keeps a snapshot of the revisions it was tested against.

  • Revised requirements during testing

    A lot keeps the versions frozen when its requirements were assigned. When a specification, a method or the rule set is revised while the lot is being tested, a quality person who recorded none of the lot's data can replace the lot's requirements with a reason and a signature. Tests whose requirement is unchanged carry over. The others stay in the record as superseded, never count, and are repeated under the new requirement. A replacement is refused while an investigation is open on the lot.

  • Requirements by product, market and customer

    A lot can fall under more than one set of requirements. Its requirements are assigned for the general scope and for each market or customer it is for, and the approved specification of each applies in full. Rule sets apply by material, stage, market and customer beside the lot's own rule set. A requirement the specifications state alike counts once. When two of them state a test differently, or something required is not configured, the lot is held: nothing is merged and neither statement is picked. A quality person who recorded none of the lot's data decides which requirement applies, with a reason and a signature, and the certificate names what applied. One deployment serves one site.

  • Lot receipt and sampling

    Lots are received into quarantine with their receipt details: the supplier, from a register kept by quality, or that the material was made on site; the supplier's and the manufacturer's lot numbers; the delivery document, date, quantity and containers; and what the examination on receipt found. A correction adds a record and keeps the original. The register records who a supplier is and does not qualify it. Lots are then assigned their specifications and sampling plan. The results on a supplier's certificate are recorded against the lot's specification, with the method and limits the certificate states, and compared with the approved limits. They stay the supplier's results, apart from the laboratory's own tests, and recording them does not replace a test. Sampling records show which containers were sampled, where, when and by whom, with deviations assessed.

  • Testing

    Numeric results and counts with units, replicates, declared rounding and batch QC checks. Qualitative results (conforms or does not conform, present or absent) are recorded with what was observed. A result reported as less than a value is kept as recorded and never becomes zero. Limits can include or exclude their boundary. A result can be calculated from raw measurements, such as weights, volumes and factors, with a formula approved with the method version; the record keeps the inputs and the formula used. Raw-data files are attached to a test and stored with a checksum. A correction adds an entry and keeps the original.

  • Out-of-specification investigations

    A failing result opens a hold and an investigation. Retests and resamples need authorization and stay linked to the original result. An independent signed conclusion closes the investigation.

  • Review and QC disposition

    A reviewer who did not record the results signs the review. Quality signs the disposition, which is blocked while tests are missing or failing or while investigations or holds are open.

  • Lot rejection and receipts made in error

    A lot in quarantine that fails its requirements or cannot be used is rejected by a quality person who recorded none of its data, with the grounds, a reason, a reference to the quality system's record and a signature. The lot need not be fully tested: the signature keeps what stood in the way of release and the tests as they stood, and the lot then takes no more test or receipt records. An open investigation is concluded first, so a failure is always investigated. A lot recorded by mistake, before anything was done with its material, is cancelled with a reason and a signature and stays in the record. What happens to rejected material is decided in the quality system.

  • Certificates of analysis

    A signed, versioned PDF built from the reviewed evidence. The issued file is stored with its checksum and returned unchanged on every download. An amendment states its reason and links to the original.

  • Contract laboratories

    A register of outside laboratories and, for each method a laboratory may perform, an approval with its evidence and dates, signed by a second quality person. A result the laboratory reports is recorded on the test with its report and is refused unless an approval covers the date of the report. It is then reviewed, investigated when it fails and printed on the certificate like any other result, with the laboratory named. The laboratory's record lists the results that relied on each approval.

  • Reliance on supplier results

    Where the laboratory's approved rule set allows it, a result stated on a supplier's certificate can stand in for a test. Quality qualifies a supplier for one material and named tests, with the evidence and dates, and a second quality person signs. An analyst proposes the stated result on the lot and a reviewer who recorded none of it signs the acceptance. The rule set lists the tests the laboratory always performs itself, such as identity. Testing and acceptance exclude each other, and the certificate of analysis lists accepted supplier results apart from the laboratory's own.

  • Equipment, reagents and qualifications

    Equipment with calibration and out-of-service status, reagent lots with release and rejection, and analyst qualifications by method. Recording a result or a batch QC check names the instruments and the reagent or standard lots used. The entry is refused when an instrument is out of calibration or out of service, a lot is expired or not released, or the analyst has no current qualification for the method. After a failed calibration the equipment page lists the results that used it.

  • Stability and retention

    Stability protocols with conditions, time points and scheduled pulls. Reserve samples, retention obligations calculated from their anchor dates, and holds that block disposal.

  • Stability testing in the system

    A protocol names the specification that states its acceptance criteria. Each pull is tested with the same recording, independent review and signatures as release testing, against the criteria in force when its tests are assigned. The tests belong to the pull, so the record of a released lot does not change. A failing result opens an investigation that holds the pull, and a reviewer who produced none of the data accepts the pull with the outcome of its results. The study shows the results by condition and time point beside the criteria. A protocol without a specification still links a sample or results kept elsewhere.

  • Signatures, audit trail and accounts

    Username and PIN are re-entered for every signature, which is bound to the exact revision signed. The audit trail records who changed what, when and why, and can be reviewed and exported. Accounts are named, roles are explicit and account changes are signed.

  • Backup and restore checks

    When a backup is taken, an operator command records each table's rows and a fingerprint of them, the schema and the migrations, read at the same instant as the database dump. After the backup is restored into a new database, a second command compares the copy with that record, recomputes the signature hashes and the checksums of issued certificates and raw-data files, and checks that the history protections are switched on. It writes a report the operator keeps as evidence of the restore test. Both commands only read the database. Schedules, off-site storage and recovery objectives stay with the laboratory.

  • Export of a lot's record

    A lot's complete record downloads as one file to keep or to hand over for an inspection: a printed copy, an electronic copy with every stored field, the raw-data files and issued certificates, and a checksum for each file. It holds the lot's receipt, samples, tests, investigations, reviews, signatures, certificates, stability records and audit trail, read at one instant. The stored checksums are checked again and the result is printed. Each export states its purpose and is recorded in the audit trail.

  • Two-factor sign-in

    Each person can add security keys or passkeys, an authenticator app on their phone, or both: after the password, sign-in asks the key, or for the code the app shows, and one-time recovery codes cover a lost key or phone. A passkey or key that checks its holder's PIN, fingerprint or face also signs in on its own, without the password, unless the laboratory turns that off. A key's answer works only at the site's own address, so a look-alike site cannot use it. Each code and each answer works once, and wrong ones count toward the same pause as wrong passwords. The laboratory chooses which roles must use it. An administrator resets it for someone else with a reason and a signature. The PIN is still re-entered for every signature.

  • Single sign-on

    A laboratory can let its staff sign in through the organization's identity provider over OpenID Connect. Each person links their own account once, after confirming their password, and the provider then signs them in without it. No account or role comes from the provider, and one organization account belongs to one laboratory account. The provider is asked to check the person at every sign-in, so a session someone left open on a shared workstation is not used. A second factor set up in the system is still asked, the PIN is still re-entered for every signature, and every link, unlink and removal is in the audit trail.

  • Validation pack of each release

    Every product requirement is traced to the automated tests that give evidence for it, and a test that is not traced fails the checks. For each commit, the automated checks build a pack: the requirements, the tests traced to each one and how they ended, the environment the tests ran in, the release notes and a checksum of every file. A laboratory can build the same pack in its own environment. The pack is evidence for the laboratory's own validation; it does not test the laboratory's configuration or intended use.

  • The laboratory's validation records

    The laboratory records the use it validated the system for: what it is used for and what is not, the supported assays, the process owner and the system owner, and its validation report with the issues found. A second quality person accepts it with a signature, which freezes the approved configuration and the release in force. The record then shows every approved version, assay or release that changed since, for the laboratory's change control; nothing is held because of it. Rehearsals of recovery from backup, of downtime with reconciliation of the records made meanwhile, and of upgrades are recorded with their criteria, outcome and evidence files, and accepted by a quality person who did not record them.

  • Documentation and deployment

    Every page has documentation inside the application. The source includes a container build, a Docker Compose profile for evaluation, automated checks and an example hosting definition.

Next

Completing the journey from lot to certificate

These areas close the gaps between what is implemented and what a laboratory needs for its routine lots. They are listed in the order we expect to take them on.

Each area names the product requirements it completes.

  • Support for validation and inspection

    Each laboratory validates the system for its own use. The validation pack of each release, the export of a lot's record and the laboratory's own acceptance and rehearsal records already supply evidence for that work. What remains:

    • Acceptance journeys for passing, failing, corrected, rejected and amended records, with the evidence the laboratory keeps.

    Requirement OQ-016

  • Expiry, retest and reassessment of material

    • A signed record that a test or an authorized retest cannot be completed, for example because its sample was lost, so that the lot can still be decided.
    • Expiry and retest dates where they apply, with a linked reassessment of material after release that preserves its original results and disposition.
    • Keep scheduled material retesting separate from an investigation's retest or resample. Reprocessing and manufacturer batch release stay in the manufacturer's quality system.

    Requirements OQ-006, OQ-008, OQ-009

  • Assess the impact of a failure

    • A quality-owned assessment of the tests, lots, stability studies and certificates affected by an equipment, reagent, supplier, contract-laboratory or other failure.
    • Keep the affected records, the decision and follow-up for each, and any protective holds linked to the assessment and to the existing quality system.
    • Signed withdrawal of an issued certificate, with its status and evidence that recipients were notified, while keeping every issued version unchanged.
    • Include problems found after release. Quality decides the response; recall, manufacturer batch release and reports to authorities stay in the manufacturer's quality system.

    Requirements OQ-008, OQ-010, OQ-011, OQ-012, OQ-014

Later

Broader laboratory capability

These areas build on the completed journey. The order is not fixed. Each one gets its own scope and a review of which rules apply before work starts.

  • Instrument data

    • Import results from instrument export files through an approved, versioned mapping.
    • Capture readings from balances and meters.
    • Keep the source file with the result and show imported values for checking before review.

  • Trending and statistics

    • Control charts and trend rules for release, stability and monitoring results.
    • Out-of-trend alerts with configurable limits, leading to an investigation.
    • Shelf-life evaluation from stability data.
    • Data export for periodic product reviews.

  • Environmental and utility monitoring

    • Sampling points, routes and schedules for rooms, surfaces, water and other utilities.
    • Alert and action limits for each point.
    • An excursion opens an investigation and can place a hold on the lots affected.

  • Specialized testing

    • Microbiology records such as plate counts, incubation readings and media checks.
    • Tests with stages and acceptance tables, such as dissolution.
    • Attribute sampling with accept and reject numbers for inspection.
    • Measurement uncertainty and decision rules for accredited laboratories.

  • Worksheets and guided methods

    • Electronic worksheets that follow the steps of the approved method.
    • Required checks, such as system suitability, completed before results are accepted.
    • The same signatures and audit trail as the rest of the record.

  • Samples, stock and storage

    • Storage locations and custody history for samples and reserve samples.
    • Sample condition on receipt, acceptance or rejection, and assessment of handling or transport deviations.
    • Preparation records, method-specific holding times and deadlines, and parent-to-aliquot lineage with quantities.
    • Storage and stability-chamber excursions linked to the samples, reserves and pulls affected, with reviewed evidence from the monitoring system.
    • Stock of reagents, standards and consumables with consumption and expiry.
    • Barcode labels and scanning.
    • Signed disposal once every retention obligation and hold has cleared.

  • Method lifecycle and training

    • Validation, verification and transfer evidence linked to each method revision.
    • Training records that feed analyst qualifications.
    • Reminders before a qualification expires.
    • Qualifications for performing, reviewing and authorizing the relevant work, beyond a person's general role.
    • The instruments, reagents and standards suitable for each method, beside their calibration, release and expiry checks.
    • Planned proficiency testing and interlaboratory comparisons where applicable, with reviewed outcomes and corrective follow-up.

  • Quality events

    • Deviations, corrective and preventive actions, change records and complaints linked to lots and results.
    • Stable links to the quality system where one already holds these records.

  • Integration with business systems

    • An interface for ERP, manufacturing and quality systems.
    • Create lots from goods receipts and production orders, and return the QC disposition.
    • Link material lots, manufacturing batches and shipments so that a lot can be traced in both directions.

  • Certificates for customers

    • Certificates for each customer from the same lot, with the customer's limits and selected tests.
    • One result judged against each specification that applies to the lot, with conformity stated for each.
    • Certificate data in a machine-readable form beside the PDF.
    • A way for the recipient to confirm that a certificate is genuine and is the current version.
    • Archival and accessible PDF output.

  • Planning and workload

    • Work lists by due date, analyst and instrument.
    • Reminders for calibrations, stability pulls and expiring reagents.
    • Turnaround and right-first-time figures.

  • Periodic review, archiving and retirement

    • Recorded periodic reviews of access, audit trails and the system, with incidents, changes and follow-up linked to the laboratory's quality system.
    • Checks that archived records remain complete, readable and retrievable throughout retention, after upgrades and after the system is retired.
    • Documented capacity limits and acceptance checks for realistic record and raw-file volumes.

  • Sites and languages

    • More than one site in a deployment.
    • A translated interface with local number and date formats.
    • Site time zones shown on records and signatures.

Exploring

Ideas under consideration

Not planned yet. An idea moves to Next or Later once it is scoped.

  • AI assistant access (MCP)

    A Model Context Protocol server, so an AI assistant can read only what the signed-in person can already see, with the same permissions. Every change still happens in the application, with its audit trail.

Outside the current plan

Saying what is not planned keeps the scope clear. These can be reconsidered when there is a concrete need.

  • Batch release

    The system records the laboratory's QC disposition. Releasing a batch to the market, including certification by a Qualified Person in the EU, stays a separate decision in the manufacturer's quality system.

  • Replacing ERP, manufacturing or document systems

    The system links to them and does not take their place.

  • Shared hosting and a customer portal

    Each deployment serves one organization, and access is for laboratory staff.

  • Clinical and nonclinical safety testing

    These follow different rules and would need their own research before they entered scope.

  • Decisions made by AI

    People record, review and sign results and dispositions. Any AI function would need its own assessment before it entered scope.
